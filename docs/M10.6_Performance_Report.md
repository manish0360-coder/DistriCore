## 1. What this document is, and the three things it keeps apart

**A failing benchmark is the most valuable one this project will produce.** B-1 says a
backup that has never been restored does not count as a backup; the same holds here. A
performance harness that has only ever reported green has proved nothing about itself.

Three categories, never merged:

1. **Measured fact** — §3, §4, §5. Timings from `ops/perf-latest.json`, unaltered.
2. **Root-cause analysis** — §7. Reasoning from the source. Stated as a hypothesis with its
   evidence, because no profiler was run.
3. **Owed to the next milestone** — §8. Not done here, and not claimed.

**Historical M7 results remain historical.** `docs/M7_Verification_Report.md` §13 measured a
different dataset on 2026-08-09, found a quadratic in the FIFO walk and fixed it. Those
numbers are correct for what they measured and are **not** restated as current anywhere
below. They are not comparable to §3: the dataset is ten times the retailers, twenty times
the SKUs and 3.6 times the ledger.

> **One recorded observation, and it is not a reason to discount this run.**
> `payments_per_customer` is M7's constant, sized for 1,000 customers. At 10,000 it yields

## 5. NFR-SCA-003 — no worse than linear · **PARTIAL**

*"No query may degrade worse than linearly with retained history"* (`02` §21.4). Fitted as
`time ~ history ** k` across 1, 3 and 5 years; linear is **k ≤ 1.0**.

| Query | 1y | 3y | 5y | k | Result |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | :-: |
| sales (customer) | 0.136 | 0.229 | 0.324 | **0.54** | OK |
| top customers | 0.166 | 0.242 | 0.287 | **0.34** | OK |
| sales (day) | 0.025 | 0.051 | 0.083 | — | NOT ASSESSABLE |
| order pipeline | 0.019 | 0.045 | 0.056 | — | NOT ASSESSABLE |
| returns | 0.009 | 0.008 | 0.009 | — | NOT ASSESSABLE |
| stock variance | 0.025 | 0.058 | 0.073 | — | NOT ASSESSABLE |

**Two of six are assessable, and both are comfortably sub-linear.** The other four fall below
the 50 ms noise floor at one year, where a growth exponent is fixed per-call overhead rather
than work. Reporting PASS over a sample that excluded two thirds of the queries would claim
coverage this instrument does not have.

`receivables ageing`, `dashboard` and `stock position` take no date range at all — their cost
scales with master data, not retained history — so this instrument cannot assess them by
construction. **NFR-SCA-003 is therefore silent on the two reports that breached**, and
nothing here should be read as clearing them.

## 7. Root cause — analysis, not measurement

**Stated as a hypothesis with its evidence.** No profiler was run; the reasoning is from the
source and from one arithmetic fact.

### 7.1 The two breaches share one cause

```
receivables ageing  17.139 s
dashboard           16.874 s
                    ─────────
difference           0.265 s
```

`dashboard()` calls `receivable_selectors.receivables_position(actor)` for its
`total_outstanding` metric; its other three figures are database aggregates.
`receivables_ageing()` calls the same function, then sorts and builds rows. The 0.265 s is
the arranging, and the 16.9 s is the selector both share.

This is the same relationship M7 recorded at a tenth of the scale — *"11.829 is 11.717 plus
three cheap figures. One root cause, two rows."*

### 7.2 What it is: Python-side full-ledger materialisation

`backend/receivables/selectors.py`, `receivables_position`:

```python
entries = list(
    CustomerLedgerEntry.objects.filter(customer__in=customers, entry_date__lte=as_of)
    .order_by("customer_id", "entry_date", "id")
)
```

At this envelope that materialises **373,000 full Django model instances** — 73,000 INVOICE
plus 300,000 PAYMENT rows, each with date and four `Decimal` conversions — before the walk
runs at all. Add 10,000 `Customer` instances, 10,000 `walk()` invocations each allocating a
dict and a set in `annulled_entry_ids`, and three dataclass allocations per open item.

### 7.3 What it is not, and why each is ruled out

| Candidate | Ruled out by |
| --- | --- |
| N+1 / per-customer query | `receivables_position` is explicitly three queries — customers, entries, credit-note map — grouped in memory. No per-customer query exists |
| Missing index | One filtered range scan on `customer__in` + `entry_date__lte`. M7 tested the index hypothesis on `top_customers` and recorded that it did not materialise; it came in at k=0.34 here |
| The quadratic M7 fixed | `ledger/walk.py` hoists `annulled_entry_ids` above the comprehension, with `Do not inline this again.` The walk is linear per party |
| Serialization / rendering | `dashboard` returns four `Metric`s and still costs 16.874 s |
| Poor join or filter shape | The same filter serves `customer statement` at 0.024 s for one customer |

### 7.4 One secondary, minor finding

`reporting/selectors.py:receivables_ageing` calls `visible_customers_for_receivables(actor)`
a **second** time to build its `names` map — a second 10,000-row query and 10,000 more model
instantiations for customers `receivables_position` already fetched and discarded. Repeated
selector work; most of the 0.265 s delta.

## 8. Owed to the next milestone — M10.6b

Not done here, and not claimed.

- **Fix the shared root cause.** One change, not two: `dashboard` has no defect of its own.
- **`ledger.walk.LedgerRow` is a `Protocol`** declaring exactly seven attributes, so a
  lightweight row satisfies the walk structurally — no adapter, no algorithm change, and
  `tests/unit/test_fifo_walk.py` unchanged. That is the shape the module was designed to
  permit.
- **Constraints that bind the fix**: M7 §7 — *"an index or a domain optimisation recorded
  with the measurement that justified it"*; **M7-4** — never a stored aggregate, never a
  reporting shortcut; **BR-005** — party balances derived from ledger entries, never stored.
  No threshold moves.
- **Re-measure and record.** This document is superseded only by a run, never by a fix.

Also open, and deliberately separate: the DR-8 profile's payment-to-invoice proportions (§2),
and NFR-SCA-003's four unassessable queries — which would need an instrument that scales
master data rather than history.

## 9. The verdict defect this run exposed

The harness computed its verdicts as a PASS test with `NOT MEASURED` as the else-branch, then
flipped every requirement to `FAIL` from one global breach counter. Both directions were
wrong, and both were reported to the operator as fact:

| Requirement | Harness printed | Truth |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| NFR-PER-003 | NOT MEASURED | **FAIL** — 13 reports timed, 2 breached |
| NFR-PER-001 | FAIL | **PARTIAL** — 11/11 S1 passed |
| NFR-SCA-001 | NOT MEASURED | **FAIL** — measured, not sustained |
| NFR-SCA-003 | FAIL | **PARTIAL** — both assessable queries sub-linear |
| NFR-PER-005 | NOT MEASURED | **MET** — the measurement plainly occurred |

**A measured breach was reported as an absence of measurement.** *Absence of a measurement is
not evidence of compliance — and a failure is not an absence.* This is the third appearance
of the same defect class in this project, after `pip-audit || true` and the restore rehearsal
that printed PASS after failing.

Fixed in M10.6a: `ops/perf_verdicts.py` holds the mapping as a pure, stdlib-only module so it
can be tested by behaviour rather than by grep, with six states — PASS, FAIL, PARTIAL,
BLOCKED, NOT MEASURED, MET — because five requirements need six. Nine contracts hold it and
each was proved able to fail.

A second defect, in the same run: `config/logging.py` sends application logs to **stdout** by
design (`00` §12), so `INFO axes.apps: AXES: BEGIN ...` landed above the opening brace and
`json.load` refused the evidence file. The harness now swaps `sys.stdout` before
`django.setup()` and writes the document to the saved stream. `ops/perf-latest.json` retains
the stray line, because it is the artefact of the run that happened.
