## 7. Root cause, and the change that answered it

### 7.1 The two breaches shared one cause

`receivables ageing` 17.139 s − `dashboard` 16.874 s = **0.265 s**. `dashboard()` called
`receivables_position` for its `total_outstanding` metric; its other three figures are
database aggregates. The 0.265 s was the arranging; the 16.9 s was the shared selector. Same
relationship M7 recorded at a tenth of the scale.

### 7.2 What it was: Python-side full-ledger materialisation

`receivables_position` built **373,000 `CustomerLedgerEntry` model instances** — field
descriptors, `_state`, deferred loading, a `from_db_value` per `Decimal` and per date — to
read the seven attributes `ledger.walk.LedgerRow` declares.

Ruled out at the time, and the re-measurement confirms each: not N+1 (three queries, none per
customer), not a missing index (`ix_cle_customer_date` already served it; `top customers`
came in at k=0.58), not the quadratic M7 fixed (hoisted, with a comment forbidding its
return), not serialization (`dashboard` returns four `Metric`s and still cost 16.874 s).

### 7.3 The change

`LedgerRowTuple` — a `NamedTuple` in `ledger/walk.py` satisfying the existing `LedgerRow`
Protocol — fed from a `values_list`, with the same `ORDER BY (customer_id, entry_date, id)`.
The algorithm, its §5A.7 invariant, its edge cases and `tests/unit/test_fifo_walk.py` are
untouched. `LedgerRow`'s members became read-only properties, which is what the walk always
did and what a `NamedTuple` requires.

The duplicate customer fetch in `receivables_ageing` and the web-admin screen was replaced by
`visible_customer_labels`, one shared selector reading two `CharField`s instead of
materialising every visible `Customer` a second time.

**No stored aggregate, no cached balance, no reporting shortcut, no threshold moved** —
M7 §7, M7-4 and BR-005 all hold. Nothing is stored; every figure is still derived by walking
immutable entries on each call.

### 7.4 Held by

`test_receivables_position_rows.py`: bulk-vs-`outstanding_for` equivalence including FIFO
order, equivalence at a historical `as_of`, `LedgerRowTuple` mirrors the Protocol,
`values_list` yields `Decimal` never `float` (P-3), `receivables_position` stays at three
queries, `receivables_ageing` at four, and labels inherit `visible_customers_for_receivables`
(BR-003). `make verify` **1241/1241**, coverage **94.57%**, mypy and ruff clean.

## 9. The verdict defect the first run exposed

M10.6a's harness computed verdicts as a PASS test with `NOT MEASURED` as the else-branch,
then flipped every requirement to `FAIL` from one global breach counter. Both directions were
wrong, and all five verdicts were wrong:

| Requirement | Harness printed | Truth |
| --- | :-: | :-: |
| NFR-PER-003 | NOT MEASURED | **FAIL** — 13 reports timed, 2 breached |
| NFR-PER-001 | FAIL | **PARTIAL** — 11/11 S1 passed |
| NFR-SCA-001 | NOT MEASURED | **FAIL** — measured, not sustained |
| NFR-SCA-003 | FAIL | **PARTIAL** — both assessable queries sub-linear |
| NFR-PER-005 | NOT MEASURED | **MET** — the measurement plainly occurred |

**A measured breach was reported as an absence of measurement.** Third appearance of that
defect class, after `pip-audit || true` and the restore rehearsal that printed PASS after
failing. Fixed in `ops/perf_verdicts.py`: a pure, stdlib-only mapping testable by behaviour,
with six states because five requirements need six.

A second defect, same run: `config/logging.py` sends application logs to stdout by design
(`00` §12), so `INFO axes.apps: AXES: BEGIN ...` landed above the opening brace and
`json.load` refused the file. stdout is now swapped before `django.setup()`. **The
2026-09-08 artefact begins at `{`** — the guard held.
